# For councils & insight teams

## Who this is for

People who already do (or should do) **insight / intelligence / performance** work: turning official statistics into evidence for members, cabinet, partners, and the public.

**Ask Ozzy is an independent civic intelligence prototype.** It visualises selected public data and helps people explore what is happening, why it might matter and what further evidence is needed. Its purpose is to support understanding and questions; democratic judgment remains with people.

## What you can explore

- A repeatable pipeline: **source → check → propose → human sign-off → publish**
- Visualisations that refuse fake precision (labelled gaps, true geography)
- A Birmingham demonstrator and playbooks intended to support future local adaptations
- Skills your technical colleagues (or AI builders) can run with you in the loop

## What you do not need

- To understand every chart library  
- To put API keys in spreadsheets  
- To accept every proposal — **Accept is a human control**

## Suggested first experiment

1. Run Ask Ozzy locally or try an available hosted version; check its selected data and limitations.
2. Pick **one** metric your members already ask about (e.g. claimants, crime, child poverty).  
3. With a builder, run the **dataset-dashboard** skill against *your* open data portal.  
4. Review the proposal together; Accept only if sources and geography are right.  
5. Share the view with one service lead; collect the next three questions.

Broader data integration, simpler reuse across councils and agentic investigation are planned directions. Begin with one useful metric; do not assume every council source will work automatically.

## Governance fit

| Control | Where it lives |
|---------|----------------|
| What may be published | Accept wall + your own review policy |
| What counts as a fact | Official sources + provenance registry |
| Who may change methods | Git history + listed fetch scripts |
| Separation of money stories | DWP benefits vs council budget — keep labelled |
